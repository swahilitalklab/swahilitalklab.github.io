The issue is not competence

It is cultural misalignment

East Africa operates on high-context relational systems where trust and interpretation matter more than process. Most international professionals are never told this — let alone given the tools to navigate it.

Meetings that end but don't move

everyone semeed to agree. The follow-through didn't come. In a high-context culture, the indirect no is an art form — and knowing the difference is the difference.

Silence misread as consent

Silence after a proposal often means careful consideration, not agreement. The professional who fills it immediately disrupts a process they don't realise is underway.

Relationships assumed, not built

Trust here is not a byproduct of working together — it is a precondition for it. Moving to business before the relationship is established signals precisely the wrong thing.

Language as relationship

A few words of Swahili, chosen well and placed correctly, signal respect, curiosity and commitment before a single agenda item is reached. That signal stays.
